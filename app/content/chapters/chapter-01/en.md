## Chronology

1. Three months after “the incident”. [[mari]] attends a mandatory session with [[therapist]] — straight from a wild night, in a filthy sweater, defending her right to self-destruct. The therapist reads it as an attempt to prove her mother no longer controls her, and sets her homework: find her own answers.
2. She texts [[rafael]], who picks her up in a Ferrari “borrowed” from a client.
3. At home she avoids her husband [[mark]]. Their son [[viktor]] is in a cast: drunk, Mari went for a neighbour woman and shoved Viktor — who tried to separate them — straight into the road.
4. After some bad molly Mari comes down on the porch, rants at a squirrel; Viktor puts her to bed.
5. Autumn. Mari tries zazen in the park. Rafael — whom she pulled into a “business” of milking rich women for money — is in prison for fraud; only the “lucky” upside-down Lucky Strike is left in the pack.
6. Mark calls: he wants a divorce — Viktor asked for it. Mari agrees for the money and dreams of “a city starting with A”.
7. In the mall parking garage she signs the papers unread. Viktor waits below by his father's car and won't look at her; Mari herself arrived in a pink BMW Z4 bought with Rafael's money.
8. Crushed by guilt — including over her parents, who died because of her — Mari smokes the lucky cigarette and drives through the garage wall.
9. The car lands on Mark and Viktor. Time stretches “like thick caramel”: Mari watches both die — and then dies herself.

> “And only darkness and silence…”

## What we learn about the world

- The stretched time at the moment of death — the first brush with [[purgatory-time]].
- Guilt over her parents' death is the root of everything that follows.
- Mari is a “hedgehog”: her cynicism turns out to be armour.
