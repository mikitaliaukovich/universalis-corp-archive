## Chronology

1. The [[archivist]] describes his eternal work: reading the [[books-of-fates]]. Perhaps Time itself wrote them; they “have always been here”.
2. He has “a couple of centuries” left — then a successor will take over.
3. His predecessor left a note: “Guard the library. And I recommend starting with this one — it's gripping” — on the fate of a virgin who conceived around 745–752 years after the founding of Rome.
4. Having just finished a vile book about a vile person, the Archivist wanders the labyrinth looking for something better.
5. The Inca section, with books bound in gold; rare golden volumes of individuals — Mansa Musa, Rockefeller. El Dorado exists, but no known soul has found it.
6. In the section on global catastrophes a book glows softly: a predicted but unfinished fate with several endings — a [[glowing-book]].
7. He sits on the floor and reads: “Her name was Mari Anna Doberstein…”

## What we learn about the world

- The [[archive]] is an endless labyrinth divided into thematic sections.
- Archivists succeed one another; the Archivist can always find his way back to his desk.
- [[mari]]'s fate is somehow linked to “global catastrophes”.
