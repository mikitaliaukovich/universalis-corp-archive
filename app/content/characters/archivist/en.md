## File

First-person narrator of his own chapters. His eternal job is to read the [[books-of-fates]] in the endless labyrinth of the [[archive]]. He suspects the books were written by Time itself and that they “have always been here”. One day a successor will take his place.

His predecessor left a two-sentence note: “Guard the library. And I recommend starting with this one — it's gripping” — lying on the book of a virgin who conceived around 745–752 years after the founding of Rome.

## The glowing book

In the section on global catastrophes the Archivist finds a softly glowing book — a predicted but unfinished fate with several possible endings ([[glowing-book]]). He sits down right on the floor and begins to read: “Her name was Mari Anna Doberstein…” ([[chapter-03]]).

:::classified{ch=5 title="Archivist 2"}
When he finishes, the Archivist is shaken: this is the first book in history altered after it was written. Handwritten edits, most events blacked out, and a new ending saying Mari will be made to do something terrible on someone else's orders. He resolves to warn her — and for the first time wonders who he really is. Tucking the book into his clutch, he sets off in search of a [[doors|door]].
:::

:::classified{ch=9 title="The Meeting"}
The Archivist steps into the world of the living at the worst possible moment: as the Milan–Zurich train hangs over the gorge, he pulls [[mari]] out of her host's body and drags her away through the door of a service compartment. He refuses to save the girl: “I'm sorry, I can't.” Had the host died with Mari inside, he explains, she would have been walled up in the dead body forever.

He introduces himself, shows her her book of fate — her name in silver on the cover, the ending blacked out — and admits he has been following her. He doesn't remember how long he has served or for which sins, but he has read thousands of fates, and only hers made him leave the [[archive]]. As far as he can tell, the fates of [[mari]], her family, [[jay-abrams|Jay Abrams]] and the passengers of the train were rewritten: their deaths were never in the plan.

Putting her account of the deal together with what he has found, the Archivist concludes that [[charon]] is steering Mari past all of [[morana]]'s systems, and that the chain of rewritten deaths threatens to bring the world down. The only way to stop it is to kill Charon — and he knows someone who can help.
:::
